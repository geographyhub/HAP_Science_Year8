How to prepare

  1. Look back through your exercise book at the tasks and questions from your lessons.
  2. Missed a lesson? Ask your teacher, or someone in your class, what you missed and catch up.
  3. Go through the BBC Bitesize pages in the topics above.
  4. Review these units on Oak National Academy: Variation, Interdependence, Earth structure and the Universe.

To get the very top marks

  1. Answer every question. A blank always gets zero. Having a go gives you a chance.
  2. Longer answers (4 to 6 marks) are marked as a whole. Link your ideas with connectives: furthermore, in addition, however, whereas, on the other hand.
  3. Don’t write “amount”. It is too vague. Write mass for a solid, volume or concentration for a liquid or solution, and number for things you count, like particles or bubbles.
  4. Graphs: plot points as small crosses in pencil. A straight line of best fit uses a ruler; a curve is drawn freehand in one smooth line.

Command words

Read the command word in each question so you know what to do.

Give / StateJust list. Only give as many as they ask for.
DescribeSay what you see, or what happens.
ExplainSay what happens, and give the scientific reason why.
SuggestUse what you know in a new situation. Often more than one answer is right, so have a go.
CompareGive similarities and differences.
EvaluateUse the numbers, add your own knowledge, link back to the question, and finish with a conclusion saying which is best and why.
